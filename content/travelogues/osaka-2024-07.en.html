This was my third time in Osaka.

I went to the same famous spots again—the same streets, the same rhythm. But this time felt different, because I came with my close friend.

The moment I remember most was one night when we ran to the supermarket, bought some wagyu, and went back to our place to cook it ourselves. We didn’t really research how to do it. I just remember the fragrant sizzle of the oil, how good the meat tasted, and how satisfied I felt afterward.

And then there was the night An and I wandered the streets for no reason at all. No destination—just walking and talking. Somehow we ended up at Don Quijote at three in the morning.

We both knew we had to fly back to Taiwan the next day, but neither of us wanted to sleep early. As if time would slow down a little, as long as we didn’t mention tomorrow.

Osaka was still Osaka—but with different people beside me, the memories became different too.
