Prague Old Town

Prague Old Town is defined by the dense layering of architectural and historical periods within a compact urban area. Medieval cobblestone streets, Gothic towers, Baroque facades, and Renaissance structures coexist seamlessly, forming a cityscape shaped by centuries of continuous development. The district is highly walkable, with streets that gradually reveal squares, churches, and courtyards.

Old Town Square

Old Town Square serves as the central gathering space of the Old Town. Surrounded by historic townhouses, churches, and civic buildings, the square maintains a balanced proportion that allows it to feel open without being overwhelming. It has long functioned as a marketplace and cultural center, retaining its importance in contemporary city life.

Astronomical Clock

The Astronomical Clock is a significant example of medieval engineering and symbolic design. Beyond telling time, it displays astronomical movements, calendar cycles, and allegorical figures. As one of the oldest functioning astronomical clocks in Europe, it reflects historical perceptions of cosmic order and timekeeping.

Charles Bridge

Charles Bridge spans the Vltava River, linking the Old Town with Malá Strana. Constructed of stone and lined with Baroque sculptures, the bridge functions both as a vital crossing and an open-air gallery. Its elevated position provides panoramic views of the river and surrounding architecture.

Prague Castle

Prague Castle is among the largest ancient castle complexes in the world. Encompassing palaces, churches, courtyards, and administrative buildings, it represents multiple architectural styles and historical eras. The castle also offers a commanding vantage point over the city, emphasizing its long-standing political and cultural significance.

St. Vitus Cathedral

Located within the castle complex, St. Vitus Cathedral is the most important religious structure in the Czech Republic. Its Gothic architecture, soaring interior, stained glass windows, and intricate stonework create a solemn and monumental atmosphere. The cathedral has historically served as a site for coronations and state ceremonies.

Malá Strana

Malá Strana lies at the foot of Prague Castle and is characterized by Baroque architecture and quiet streets. Compared to the Old Town, the area feels more residential and intimate, with gardens, small churches, and preserved 17th- and 18th-century buildings contributing to its distinctive charm.
