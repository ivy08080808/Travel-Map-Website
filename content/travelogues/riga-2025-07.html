Riga Old Town

Riga Old Town's charm lies not in a single architectural style, but in the layers of time that have accumulated over centuries. The narrow medieval cobblestone streets, exquisitely decorated facades with arched windows, and numerous elegant yet understated Art Nouveau buildings create a unique atmosphere. The buildings are painted in soft beige, light blue, and pink-orange hues that appear particularly warm in the sunlight. Walking through the old town feels like being embraced by the city rather than overwhelmed by landmarks. With few tourists, perfect temperature and sunshine, the overall experience is truly delightful.

House of the Blackheads

The House of the Blackheads was originally a meeting place for the Brotherhood of Blackheads, a medieval guild of unmarried merchants. Its exterior, constructed with red brick and white decorations, is magnificent. Destroyed during World War II, what we see today is a faithful reconstruction that restored the building to its original appearance. This building is often regarded as a symbol of Riga's Old Town and strongly represents the city's historical and commercial traditions.

Laima Clock

The Laima Clock is one of Riga's most famous meeting points, also known as "Riga's dating landmark." The clock features the Laima chocolate logo, symbolizing an important Latvian local brand and daily life in the city. It stands as a beloved symbol of Riga's urban culture and serves as a popular gathering spot for locals and visitors alike.

Riga Central Market

The Riga Central Market was converted from old Zeppelin hangars and is one of the largest markets in Europe. The market displays various breads, smoked meats, cheeses, fish, and fresh fruits. Prices are affordable and the atmosphere is authentic. Visiting the market is not just about shopping but also about experiencing the city's daily rhythm. The strawberries are particularly noteworthy—very sweet and large. I bought a bag to eat at the market and found them very fragrant.

Cat House

The Cat House is one of Riga's representative Art Nouveau style buildings. The black cat sculptures on the roof are the most famous feature of this building. Legend has it that the owner originally wanted to join the Great Guild across the street but was rejected. In a fit of anger, he placed two black cat sculptures on the roof, intentionally positioning their backsides towards the Guild building as a public provocation. This act caused considerable controversy at the time and became a topic of discussion in the city. Later, after the owner successfully joined the Guild, the cat sculptures were turned to face the street, where they remain today.

Freedom Monument

The Freedom Monument is one of Latvia's most important national symbols, located in the center of Riga. Completed in 1935, it commemorates those who sacrificed their lives in the Latvian War of Independence. At its top is a female statue holding three golden stars, symbolizing Latvia's three historical regions and national freedom. Remarkably, it was preserved even during the Soviet era, making it an important symbol of national identity. To this day, it remains a significant place for national commemorative events and public expressions of respect.

Riga Central Station Clock Tower (Origo) & National Library of Latvia

The National Library of Latvia, also known as "Castle of Light," is one of Riga's most representative modern buildings. Its shape resembles a sloping mountain, symbolizing the accumulation of knowledge and national culture. That evening, I went out for a walk and watched the sunset. The entire city was bathed in purple and orange hues, truly beautiful.
