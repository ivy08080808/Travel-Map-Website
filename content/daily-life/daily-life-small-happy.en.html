Just Tiny Joys Are Enough to Support This Vast and Desolate Life

After reading the entire book, my deepest feeling is: Life is not made up of grand events, but rather by small, everyday things slowly stringing together.

Those seemingly ordinary fragments—a conversation, a familiar path, a sudden memory—accumulate over time, slowly forming our lives, progressing "day by day, little by little."

I realize that life isn't about constant brilliance, but about finding "a little bit of light" in the long, repetitive days—whether from past memories or present moments of tenderness.

Even if the world seems desolate, even if progress is slow, as long as one can feel these "tiny joys," life can still be sustained. Through this understanding, I have slowly accepted the "weight and meaning of everyday life."
