Lee Garden Hotel has been demolished for many years, rebuilt into a shopping mall; those few herbal tea shops in Mong Kok have also become 3C product stores; many bookstores on the second floor have closed. I tried to recall with my slightly demented mother our past adventures in Hong Kong. She couldn't respond, a bewildered expression appearing on her face. I knew all this was too difficult for her. She apologized for not remembering anything, so I said it didn't matter, it really didn't, and gave her a gentle, understanding smile.

I often feel that the MRT or subway is the speed of the city, quickly taking us to our destination, and also quickly leaving the past far behind.

This book made me rethink the essence of life. Life may not be composed of grand moments, but rather countless small moments stacked together. In what seems like a desolate life, those small joys—a cup of hot tea, a smile, a memory—are enough to support us through the long journey.

When we cannot change our circumstances, we can at least choose how to face them. Like those memories taken away by time in the book, though they cannot be relived, those small joys can light a lamp for us in the desolation.
