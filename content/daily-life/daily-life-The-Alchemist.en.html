This is one of the things that makes travel so appealing: you get to meet many new people, without having to devote too much of your time to any of them.

When you spend every day with the same group of people, they eventually become part of your life. And then they begin to want you to change to suit their expectations. If you are not what they want you to be, they become upset.

Almost everyone seems to know exactly how others should live their lives, yet knows nothing about how to live their own.

The Alchemist

"I live on because of the idea of going to Mecca. It is that idea which gives me the strength to face the same unchanging days, to endure the silent water pipes, and the endless meals of lunch and dinner at this dreadful café.

I am afraid that once my dream is fulfilled, I will have no reason left to go on living.

You dream of your sheep and your pyramids, but you and I are different. You want to realize your dream. I only want to dream of Mecca."

The Alchemist

These are my two favorite sentences in the whole book.

Many times, what truly supports us to keep going, is not whether the dream will come true, but that we always keep a dream in our hearts.

That dream makes day-to-day life bearable, giving direction and meaning to ordinary, even somewhat silent days.

Perhaps a dream doesn't necessarily have to be completed, but as long as it exists in our hearts, we still have a reason to move forward.
